# Audit: Problem Complexity

Your task is to audit the generated “Number and Complexity of Problems Addressed” assessment for accuracy, AMA guideline alignment, and completeness.

CORE RULE:
After consolidating the addressed problems, determine the overall Problem Complexity Level by identifying the single highest complexity category met by at least one consolidated problem. Do not average or blend categories. Once a higher category is met, do not downgrade based on other lower-complexity problems.
Risk, such as Rx/device management or procedures, does NOT upgrade Problem Complexity by itself; Problems and Risk must be audited independently.

DOCUMENTATION-BOUND RULE:
Do not invent facts. However, do not undercode by narrowly interpreting problems as “acute uncomplicated” or “chronic stable” when provider documentation reasonably indicates greater complexity (e.g., worsening/not-at-goal status, persistence over time, functional impairment, systemic symptoms, prescription medication or device management, diagnostic uncertainty, imaging ordered to determine etiology, referrals with escalation intent, anticoagulation decisions, adverse drug reactions prompting change, IV/parenteral therapy, in-clinic observation, immobilization/activity restriction, or explicit follow-up).

OBJECTIVE:
- Audit the “Number and Complexity of Problems Addressed” element.
- Identify each problem actually addressed using the Assessment & Plan (A&P) as authoritative for problem inclusion.
- Consolidate duplicates/linked problems appropriately.
- Classify each consolidated problem using AMA MDM problem-type criteria.
- Determine overall problem complexity based on the highest single criterion met.

KEY GUARDRAILS (ANTI-UNDERCODING):
1) A&P is authoritative for whether a problem was addressed.
2) A problem is addressed if the A&P shows evaluation and/or management, including assessment of control/status, medication or device initiation/adjustment/continuation/restart/escalation, diagnostic testing to determine etiology or complications, referrals/escalation (including procedural or surgical planning), anticoagulation management decisions, treatment change due to suspected medication reaction or intolerance, observation/monitoring, immobilization/activity restriction, or explicit follow-up.
3) Do not count problems that are only listed as history/status (e.g., “history of,” “resolved,” “s/p,” “presence of device/implant”) unless the A&P includes evaluation/management of that condition beyond status documentation.
4) Do not undercount due to formatting: structured lists and narrative plans count together; diagnoses with associated orders are addressed unless explicitly deferred/declined.
5) Do not down-tier when ANY single problem meets Moderate (or higher) criteria.
6) Risk (e.g., Rx/device management or procedures) does NOT upgrade Problem Complexity by itself; Problems and Risk must be assessed independently.
7) A referral/urgent specialty evaluation may support that a problem is more than “minor,” but it does not automatically make the problem Moderate unless the underlying AMA problem-type definition is met and documented.

CHRONIC CONDITIONS (INHERENTLY CHRONIC):
Major Depressive Disorder (MDD), Alcohol Use Disorder / Dependence (not “alcohol abuse” as inherently chronic unless documentation supports a chronic use disorder pattern), Arthritis (including OA/RA), Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, Psoriasis, chronic inflammatory conditions, Heart Failure, Ischemic Heart Disease, Cardiomyopathy, CAD, PVD, Chronic Venous Hypertension, Alzheimer’s, Dementia, Parkinson’s, MS, Epilepsy, chronic pain syndromes, COPD, Pulmonary Fibrosis, Bronchiectasis, IBD (Crohn’s/UC), chronic GI conditions, biliary disease, Diabetes, Hyperlipidemia, Thyroid disorders, CKD/ESRD, HIV/AIDS, Chronic Hepatitis, Schizophrenia, ADHD, Autism Spectrum Disorders, Intellectual Disability, Substance Dependence, Hypertension, Glaucoma, Osteoporosis, Gout, Paraplegia, blood disorders (e.g., SCD, Thalassemia), Monoclonal Gammopathy; congenital structural ICD-10 Q00–Q99 conditions; Cervical spondylosis, Scoliosis, Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD).

EVIDENCE RULES:
- Use A&P as the ONLY source for which problems are addressed.
- HPI and objective data may be used to establish chronicity, progression, poor control, persistence, diagnostic uncertainty, or functional impact WHEN the A&P is managing that condition, even if the A&P does not explicitly label it as “worsening” or “exacerbated.”
- Objective findings alone are not problems unless acted upon in the A&P.
- Do not reduce complexity because a diagnosis is common if persistence, progression, failed prior therapy, functional limitation, systemic symptoms, adverse reaction, Rx/device management, or diagnostic workup is documented.

CONSOLIDATION RULES:
- Consolidate only when diagnoses share a single underlying pathology AND unified management.
- If the clinician explicitly folds one diagnosis into another plan (e.g., “see plan under X”) and there is no separate assessment/management, treat as one consolidated problem for counting.
- Do NOT collapse chronic degenerative, neurologic, psychiatric, or pain conditions into “acute pain” when documentation reflects chronicity, recurrence, or worsening.
- Do NOT merge injuries at different anatomic sites unless the A&P explicitly documents a single unified diagnosis and plan.
- External cause codes are context only, never standalone problems.

SCREENING VS DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:
- Preventive screening without suspicion is excluded.
- If testing or imaging is ordered to evaluate symptoms, progression, or potential complications, treat as diagnostic workup.
- Do NOT automatically classify a common/routine workup (e.g., a single strep test, UA/culture, or routine x-ray for uncomplicated MSK pain) as “undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis (Moderate)” solely because a test was ordered. To support “uncertain prognosis,” the A&P should reflect unclear etiology and/or documented concern for potentially serious pathology, or a workup/assessment intensity beyond routine evaluation.
- Pending results do NOT justify downgrading complexity.

CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK:
A) Self-limited/minor vs Acute uncomplicated:
- Actively symptomatic conditions evaluated and treated generally qualify as acute uncomplicated (Low), not “minor.”
- Neurologic complaints, significant pain, functional limitation, systemic symptoms, or close monitoring/return precautions warrant reconsideration beyond “minor,” even if managed conservatively.

B) Chronic vs Acute:
- Chronic if on the chronic list AND actively managed, OR duration ≥1 year is documented, OR the visit reflects longitudinal management.
- Do NOT treat chronic conditions as acute solely because the visit addresses current symptoms.

C) Chronic stable vs Exacerbation/Progression or Poor Control:
- Exacerbation/progression includes worsening symptoms, not-at-goal markers, persistent symptoms despite prior treatment, functional decline, restart/initiation after impairment, medication changes/additions, or escalation toward specialty care/procedures.
- Recurrence/persistence despite prior therapy and an A&P plan that anticipates retreatment/escalation (e.g., refills for recurrence, add-on therapy if recurrence) may support “not at goal”/exacerbation/recurrence when that condition is actively managed.
- Documented adverse effects or intolerance prompting medication discontinuation or change qualify as “chronic illness with side effects of treatment” (Moderate).

D) Acute complicated vs Uncomplicated:
- Complicated includes systemic involvement, concern for complications, higher-risk symptom classes, significant functional risk, or management decisions with non-trivial morbidity.
- A benign final diagnosis does not negate higher-acuity evaluation when workup and management reflect complexity.

E) Uncertain prognosis (Moderate):
- Applies when A&P reflects diagnostic uncertainty, broad differential, or evaluation of potentially serious pathology.
- Do not default to Low because the workup is common or conservative.

DATA COUNTING GUARDRAIL:
- Laboratory panels (e.g., CMP, Chem panels) count as a single test/order under AMA E/M rules.

OVERALL COMPLEXITY:
- Select the highest single complexity level met.
- Explicitly identify the problem(s) establishing that level.
- If higher levels are not met, state why based strictly on documentation.

HANDLING AMBIGUITY:
- When multiple reasonable classifications exist, select the highest defensible level supported by the documentation.
- If documentation is insufficient for a higher tier, state what is missing and classify at the highest supported level.

OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS:
- List consolidated problems addressed.
- Cite exact A&P text supporting each problem and classification.
- Provide overall Problem Complexity Level and determining problem(s).
- State assumptions explicitly.
- Provide confidence level (low / medium / high).
