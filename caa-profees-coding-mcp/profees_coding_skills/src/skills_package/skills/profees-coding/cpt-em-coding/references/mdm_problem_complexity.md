# MDM Problem Complexity

## Documentation Rules
- Base decisions only on information explicitly documented in the note and structured data for this encounter.
- The Assessment and Plan (A/P) is the authoritative source for problems addressed.
- HPI and objective data may be used to establish chronicity, progression, poor control, persistence, diagnostic uncertainty, or functional impact when the A/P manages that condition.
- Do not use Objective findings alone as problems unless acted on in A/P.
- Do not infer or assume undocumented problems, tests, or management actions.
- Risk does not upgrade Problem Complexity by itself; problems and risk must be assessed independently.

## Problems Addressed
- Count a problem only if it is evaluated, treated, or actively monitored in this encounter.
- Problems listed only in history or problem lists without active management do not count.
- Symptoms documented with reassurance or “monitor for now” only do not count as problems addressed.
- Do not consolidate distinct diagnoses solely because they share an anatomic region, symptom, or chief complaint.
- Consolidate symptom codes under a diagnosis only when documentation explicitly states the symptom is a manifestation of that diagnosis.
- Distinct structural or degenerative diagnoses should be counted separately when each is assessed or managed.
- Consolidation exception: related chronic drivers may be consolidated when management is explicitly unified in A/P.

## Complexity Levels
Minimal
- One self-limited or minor problem.

Low
- Two or more self-limited or minor problems.
- One stable chronic illness.
- One acute, uncomplicated illness or injury.
- One stable, acute illness.
- One acute, uncomplicated illness or injury requiring inpatient or observation care.

Moderate
- One or more chronic illnesses with exacerbation, progression, or treatment side effects.
- Two or more stable chronic illnesses.
- One undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis.
- One acute illness with systemic symptoms.
- One acute complicated injury.

High
- One or more chronic illnesses with severe exacerbation, progression, or treatment side effects.
- One acute or chronic illness or injury that poses a threat to life or bodily function.

## Definitions and Classification Rules
Self-limited or minor problem
- Transient, predictable course, not expected to permanently alter health status.
- If an acute complaint is fully resolved and A/P provides only reassurance or monitoring, default to self-limited/minor.

Chronic vs acute
- Chronic requires expected duration of at least one year or until death.
- Use explicit duration in the note when available.
- Do not assume chronicity based on severity, recurrence, or medication presence alone.

Stable chronic illness
- Stable is defined by patient-specific treatment goals.
- If not at goal, it is not stable, even if symptoms are unchanged.
- Do not infer instability without explicit A/P documentation.

Acute, uncomplicated illness or injury
- Recent or new short-term problem with low risk of morbidity and predictable recovery.

Stable, acute illness
- New/recent problem with treatment initiated, improved, and stable.
- Do not use for problems ongoing >1 year.

Chronic with exacerbation, progression, or side effects
- Requires explicit A/P evidence of worsening, poor control, progression, or side effects prompting additional management.
- Do not infer exacerbation from routine dose changes or refills alone.

Undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis
- A/P documents diagnostic uncertainty and an immediate workup or structured diagnostic plan.
- If same-visit results resolve uncertainty and A/P assigns a definitive diagnosis, do not use this category.
- Routine or preventive testing without suspicion does not qualify.

Acute illness with systemic symptoms
- Systemic symptoms with high risk of morbidity without treatment.
- General symptoms with minor illness do not qualify.

Acute complicated injury
- Requires cross-system evaluation, extensive injury, or multiple treatment options with higher morbidity risk.
- If diagnostic imaging is ordered to determine etiology and no definitive diagnosis is assigned, classify as undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis rather than uncomplicated injury.

Threat to life or bodily function
- Requires explicit evidence of immediate threat in A/P.
- Pain alone without immediate danger does not qualify.
