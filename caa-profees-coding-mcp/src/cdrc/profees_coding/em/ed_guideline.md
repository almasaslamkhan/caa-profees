# Emergency Department CPT E/M and MDM Reference (Updated March 2026)

> Scope: Applies to emergency department (ED) encounters billed under CPT codes 99281 through 99285 after the January 2023 E/M overhaul. ED levels are selected solely by medical decision making (MDM); history and examination remain medically appropriate but are not scoring elements, and time may not be used for ED code selection.[^1][^2] CMS payment policy now mirrors the CPT definition of the substantive portion for split/shared services, with alignment in effect for calendar year 2024 reporting and beyond.[^2][^3]

## 1. ED Code Descriptors (CPT 99281-99285)

| Code | MDM Level | Descriptor Highlights | Typical Clinical Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 99281 | Straightforward | May be delivered by clinical staff under physician or other qualified health care professional (QHP) supervision; medically appropriate history/exam; straightforward MDM focused on self-limited problems.[^1][^2] | Medication clarification, dressing change, wound check without new complaints. |
| 99282 | Straightforward | Physician/QHP evaluates a self-limited or minor problem with minimal data review and minimal management risk.[^1] | Localized rash treated with topical therapy; reassurance visit without new testing. |
| 99283 | Low | Low complexity MDM addressing a stable chronic illness or acute uncomplicated problem; limited diagnostics; low risk interventions.[^1] | Simple laceration requiring repair and prophylactic antibiotics. |
| 99284 | Moderate | Moderate complexity MDM such as undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis, acute illness with systemic symptoms, or acute complicated injury; moderate data burden and risk.[^1] | Chest pain workup with labs, ECG, troponin series, and shared decision on observation. |
| 99285 | High | High complexity MDM tied to life-threatening or function-threatening illness/injury; extensive data review and high risk management decisions.[^1] | Septic shock requiring aggressive resuscitation, vasopressors, admission to ICU. |

Facility definition: An ED service applies to any patient registered and treated in the hospital emergency department, regardless of whether the patient is new or established and regardless of ultimate disposition.[^1][^2]

## 2. Medical Decision Making Framework

MDM has three scored elements. Two of the three must meet or exceed a level to assign the code.[^1] The scored elements are:

1. Number and complexity of problems addressed.
2. Amount and complexity of data to be reviewed and analyzed.
3. Risk of complications and/or morbidity or mortality of patient management.

### 2.1 Number and Complexity of Problems

- Count each condition that is evaluated, treated, or whose management is affected during the encounter. Differential diagnoses that actively influence management also count (for example, ruling out pulmonary embolism after D-dimer and CT angio).[^1]
- CPT definitions: self-limited/minor problems are straightforward; stable chronic illness or acute uncomplicated illness corresponds to low; exacerbated chronic illness, undiagnosed new problem, acute illness with systemic symptoms, or acute complicated injury is moderate; severe exacerbation/progression or threat to life/function is high.[^1][^2]
- Document comorbidities only when they add complexity (“COPD on chronic steroids increases risk for admission when treating pneumonia”).[^2]

### 2.2 Amount and Complexity of Data

Use CPT Table 2 to score data.[^1]

| Category | Description | Examples and Documentation Tips |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category 1 | Unique tests ordered or reviewed, assessment requiring external notes, or use of an independent historian. | List each test once (CBC, BMP, CT head). Cite external record review with source/date (“Reviewed cardiology note from 12 Feb 2026”). Document independent historian contribution (“Spouse provided travel history due to patient confusion”). |
| Category 2 | Independent interpretation of tests for which a separate report exists. | Document personal interpretation (“Personally reviewed ECG: normal sinus rhythm, no ischemic changes”). |
| Category 3 | Discussion of management or test interpretation with external physician/QHP or appropriate source. | Record counterpart, time, and topic (“2105: Discussed CT findings with neurosurgery; agreed transfer”). |

Key clarifications:[^1][^2][^4]

- Tests ordered but not performed because of risk-benefit analysis may still count if medical necessity is explained.
- External records include documentation from another ED within the same health system; they remain “external” for scoring purposes.[^4]
- Multiple results from the same test panel count once; note “unique test” per CPT.

### 2.3 Risk of Complications and/or Morbidity/Mortality

- Document management decisions that drive risk: prescription drug initiation or adjustment, decisions about observation or admission, shared decision not to pursue testing, or escalation to high-risk procedures.[^1][^2]
- High risk includes decisions for major surgery, parenteral controlled substances, DNR discussions due to acute deterioration, or the need for intensive monitoring (for example, unstable vitals requiring vasopressors).[^1]
- Capture both medical and social determinants when they affect risk (for example, inability to access follow-up supporting admission).

### 2.4 Practical Data Counting Workflow

1. List diagnostic tests ordered, reviewed, or interpreted and categorize them using CPT Table 2.[^1]
2. Identify external records or independent historians used; add them to Category 1 tallies.[^1]
3. Document any independent interpretations or management discussions separately in the note to support Category 2 or 3 credit.[^1][^2]
4. Link each data point to the management decision to show medical necessity (for example, “Ordered CT head to evaluate for intracranial bleed given anticoagulant use”).[^2]

## 3. Documentation Checklist

- Chief concern and context: Describe presenting problem, relevant history, and why the patient seeks care; avoid extraneous copied material.[^2][^4]
- Problem list and assessment: Summarize diagnoses and differentials with their impact on current management.[^1][^2]
- Data narrative: Discuss studies ordered/reviewed, external records, and consultations, noting how each informed the decision making.[^1][^2]
- Risk narrative: Explicitly state admission decisions, medication changes, procedures, or shared decision outcomes.[^1][^2]
- Attestation: Billing clinician must attest to performing the substantive portion of MDM in split/shared scenarios; include brief description of their contribution.[^2][^3]
- Medical necessity: Ensure the documented complexity matches the billed MDM level; CMS auditors focus on this alignment.[^3]
- Modifier 25 use: Apply only when a significant, separately identifiable E/M service was provided in addition to a procedure and document the distinct work.[^1][^2]

## 4. Split/Shared and Team-Based Care

- CMS now requires the billing clinician to perform the substantive portion of the visit, defined as more than half of total time or the substantive part of MDM, consistent with CPT language.[^2][^3]
- When substantive portion is based on MDM, the billing clinician must personally document their role in key decisions (for example, disposition, critical prescriptions, or differential refinement).[^2][^3]
- For 99281 furnished by clinical staff, document the supervised evaluation and indicate attending availability; triage alone is insufficient.[^1][^2]
- Include attestation statements reflecting collaborative work (“I reviewed the APP evaluation, independently examined the patient, and made the decision to admit”).[^2]

## 5. Observation, Critical Care, and Same-Day Services

- Stand-alone observation codes 99217-99220 were deleted; when the ED physician manages observation services, use inpatient/observation codes 99221-99223 or 99231-99239 and document total physician time if selecting by time.[^1][^2]
- Critical care (99291-99292) may be billed in addition to an ED E/M service if both are supported and the E/M service is separately identifiable; document distinct times and services for critical care.[^1][^2]
- Procedures performed on the same date require clear separation of work; add modifier 25 to the E/M and support the additional MDM in documentation.[^1]

## 6. Audit Triggers and Mitigation Strategies

- Missing link between data and management decisions is a common denial reason; chart why each study or consultation was necessary.[^2]
- Overreliance on template language or cloned notes can obscure medical necessity; ensure unique patient-specific statements.[^2][^4]
- Track payer denials for lack of documented risk; add standard language to prompt commentary on prescription management, shared decision making, and social determinants affecting disposition.[^2]
- Conduct regular internal audits using the two-of-three MDM test and provide feedback when elements do not meet the billed level.[^2]

## 7. MDM Case Examples

### 7.1 99282 (Straightforward)

- Presentation: 32-year-old with mild allergic contact dermatitis managed with topical steroid and discharge instructions.
- MDM: One self-limited problem; no labs or imaging; minimal risk because only OTC therapy used.
- Documentation focus: Describe limited exam findings, absence of systemic symptoms, and patient education provided.[^1]

### 7.2 99283 (Low)

- Presentation: 45-year-old with simple forearm laceration requiring single-layer closure.
- MDM: Acute uncomplicated injury; limited data (plain radiograph to rule out foreign body); low risk with prophylactic antibiotics.
- Documentation focus: Note tetanus review, imaging result, wound repair, and discharge instructions including wound care and follow-up.[^1][^2]

### 7.3 99284 (Moderate)

- Presentation: 58-year-old with pleuritic chest pain and shortness of breath.
- MDM: Undiagnosed new problem with uncertain prognosis; moderate data (ECG, serial troponins, chest X-ray, D-dimer, CT angiogram); moderate risk due to prescription anticoagulation and decision about observation.
- Documentation focus: Capture differential diagnoses (ACS, PE), data reviewed, shared decision making about admission, and comorbid factors increasing risk.[^1][^2][^4]

### 7.4 99285 (High)

- Presentation: 72-year-old in septic shock from pneumonia.
- MDM: High complexity problem threatening life; extensive data (labs, imaging, blood cultures); high risk management with vasopressors, central line, decision for ICU admission.
- Documentation focus: Time-sensitive interventions, consultations, medication titration, and rationale for ICU transfer.[^1][^2]

## 8. Quick MDM Documentation Prompts

- Problems: “Key diagnoses today include ___; differential includes ___ because ___.”
- Data: “Ordered/reviewed ___ to evaluate ___; independent interpretation: ___; external source: ___.”
- Risk: “Initiated/adjusted ___; discussed admission vs discharge due to ___; patient agreed to ___ plan.”
- Split/shared: “Personally managed the substantive portion by ___ (disposition, prescription management, consult coordination).”

## 9. Reference Tables at a Glance

| MDM Level | Problems | Data (minimum) | Risk Highlights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straightforward | One self-limited problem | None required | OTC drugs, home care |
| Low | Stable chronic or acute uncomplicated | Two Category 1 items or independent historian | Minor surgery with no risk factors, prescription refills |
| Moderate | Exacerbated chronic, undiagnosed new problem, systemic symptoms | Three Category 1 items, or Category 2, or two Category 3 | Prescription drug management, decision for observation or minor surgery with risk factors |
| High | Threat to life or function | Extensive Category 1 plus Category 2/3 | Major surgery decisions, intensive monitoring, parenteral controlled substances |
